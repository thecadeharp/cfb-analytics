The canonical THI statement

What The Hammer Index Is—
and What It Isn’t

THI exists to create an independent view of college football and college basketball. The standard is not certainty. It is a useful forecast, a documented process and an honest record that stays visible when the model is wrong.

Benchmark CenterPublic Changelog
01

Why THI exists

THI turns team, player, schedule and game data into independent ratings and projections. It is built for people who want to understand a matchup—not simply inherit a poll, a market number or a narrative.

02

Power is not résumé

Predictive power asks how strong a team is expected to be going forward. Résumé evaluates what a team has accomplished. Both matter, but they answer different questions and should not be forced into the same ranking.

03

A forecast is not a bet

Projection error and market performance are reported separately. A model can forecast scores well without producing a tradable edge, and a short ATS run does not prove forecasting quality. THI publishes both views without pretending they are interchangeable.

04

Frozen means frozen

Prospective forecasts and tracked decisions are retained before results are known. Settlement joins the final score later. Corrections are documented; weak outcomes are not replaced with better-looking numbers.

05

Production and research are different

CFB Model A is a production system. CBB is currently a research environment with sample-gated signals. Research outputs can be useful, but they do not receive production language until their stated promotion contract clears.

06

Availability is disclosed, not disguised

Injury and lineup information can materially change a game. THI distinguishes model-priced adjustments, public context and signal holds. A hold is a governance decision, not permission to rewrite the original forecast or erase a settled result.

07

What THI currently does poorly

Samples remain young. Some confidence tiers do not yet beat the market consistently. CBB spread and probability calibration still need more prospective evidence. Market-source coverage varies, and older CBB projections do not all have archived freeze timestamps. Those limits remain public.

08

What would change the methodology

A methodology change requires evidence, documented testing, a version boundary and a prospective start date. Model weights, market rules and presentation changes receive separate changelog labels so cosmetic work cannot reset a record.

09

No guarantees

THI is an analytical baseline, not a promise of profit or a “lock” service. Uncertainty, price, timing and personal risk tolerance matter. Users remain responsible for their decisions and should never wager more than they can afford to lose.

10

Corrections are part of the work

Data errors and edge cases will occur. THI corrects verified mistakes, documents material effects and preserves the distinction between a data correction and a model change. The goal is not to appear infallible; it is to remain auditable.
